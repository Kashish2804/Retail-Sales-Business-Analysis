# Business Recommendations

Each recommendation is linked to a specific, evidence-based finding from
`insights.md`.

---

### 1. Cap routine promotional discounts at 20%
**What:** Set a standard ceiling of 20% off for regular promotions; reserve
30%-off events for genuine clearance/inventory-clearing purposes only.
**Why:** The 30% discount tier produces a **negative average profit margin
(-0.6%)** — the business loses money on average at that discount depth.
**Supporting finding:** Insight #4 (Discounting above 15% erodes
profitability).

### 2. Review Electronics pricing and cost structure
**What:** Audit unit pricing, supplier costs, and discount frequency across
the Electronics category, especially Bluetooth Speaker and Laptop Stand.
**Why:** Electronics generates the most revenue (₹27.68L) but the lowest
margin of any category (10.1%) — the biggest category is also the least
efficient one.
**Supporting finding:** Insights #1, #2, and #6.

### 3. Reassess USB-C Charger's place in the catalog
**What:** Either reposition USB-C Charger as a bundled add-on (sold with
other Electronics) rather than a standalone product, or renegotiate its
cost basis.
**Why:** It is weak on both dimensions — low sales (₹70,024) and the lowest
margin of any product (3.2%) — so it isn't earning its place either as a
volume driver or a profit driver.
**Supporting finding:** Insight #5.

### 4. Shift marketing/inventory investment toward Furniture and Home & Kitchen
**What:** Direct incremental promotional spend and stock investment toward
these two categories rather than spreading it evenly across all five.
**Why:** They combine meaningful sales volume with healthy margins (20.7%
and 33.8% respectively) — a better return profile than Electronics
(margin-constrained) or Stationery (volume-constrained).
**Supporting finding:** Insight #10.

### 5. Plan inventory and staffing ahead of the October–November peak
**What:** Increase stock levels and marketing activity in September, ahead
of the demonstrated Oct–Nov demand spike, and treat January's dip as
expected rather than a problem to solve.
**Why:** October and November are the two highest-revenue months in the
dataset by a clear margin, consistent with a seasonal/festive demand
pattern.
**Supporting finding:** Insight #3.

### 6. Build a retention program around the existing repeat-customer base
**What:** Introduce a simple loyalty or repeat-purchase incentive (e.g.
a discount on a customer's 3rd+ order) rather than concentrating budget on
new-customer acquisition alone.
**Why:** 81% of customers (314 of 386) are already repeat buyers — retention
is evidently the stronger lever than acquisition in this business.
**Supporting finding:** Insight #8.

### 7. Investigate the one-time-buyer segment
**What:** Pull a short follow-up analysis (or simple survey) on the 72
one-time customers to understand why they didn't return — product fit,
price, delivery experience, etc.
**Why:** Understanding this smaller segment could convert some of them into
the repeat-buyer pattern that already drives most of the business.
**Supporting finding:** Insight #8 (and the gap it leaves unanswered).

---

*Recommendations are intentionally limited to actions the data directly
supports. Recommendations reserved for the Intermediate-level project
(e.g. RFM-based segmentation, churn modeling, predictive reorder
timing) are out of scope here by design.*
