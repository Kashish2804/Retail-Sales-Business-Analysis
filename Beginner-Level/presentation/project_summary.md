# 3–5 Minute Video Script — Retail Sales & Business Performance Dashboard

**0:00–0:30 — Introduction**
"Hi, I'm [your name], and this is my Beginner-Level Data Analyst internship
project: a Retail Sales & Business Performance Analysis Dashboard. The
business problem I'm answering is: how is the retail business performing,
and which products, categories, and regions are driving revenue and profit?"

**0:30–1:00 — Dataset**
"I used a synthetic retail sales dataset built to mirror a realistic
e-commerce export — I want to be upfront that it's synthetic, not real
company data. It covers 15 months, January 2024 through March 2025, with
just over 2,200 raw transactions across 5 product categories, 4 regions,
and 386 customers."

**1:00–1:30 — Data Cleaning**
"Before analysis, I inspected the raw data and found some realistic
issues: 25 duplicate rows, some invalid dates, a handful of invalid
quantity or price entries, and inconsistent spelling in the Category and
Region fields. I documented every cleaning decision — for example, missing
discount values were filled with 0% rather than dropped, since no discount
recorded just means none was applied. After cleaning, I had 2,189 reliable
records to work with."

**1:30–2:15 — KPIs & Analysis**
"From there I calculated the core KPIs — Total Sales, Total Profit, Total
Orders, Average Order Value, and Profit Margin — all as live Excel
formulas. Then I broke the data down six ways: monthly trends, category
performance, product performance, regional performance, customer behavior,
and profitability specifically — because as I'll show, high sales doesn't
always mean high profit."

**2:15–3:15 — Dashboard Walkthrough**
[Share your screen on the Dashboard tab]
"This is the dashboard. Up top are six KPI cards — Sales, Profit, Orders,
Customers, Average Order Value, and Profit Margin. These three dropdowns
let you filter by Year, Region, and Category, and the cards update live.
Below that are four charts: the monthly sales and profit trend, category
comparison, regional comparison, and my top 10 products by sales."

**3:15–4:15 — Key Findings**
"A few things stood out. Electronics is my top category by sales, but it
actually has the lowest profit margin of any category — just over 10%,
compared to nearly 49% for Stationery. Discounting also matters a lot:
margin steadily drops as discount depth increases, and at 30% off, the
average sale actually loses money. And on the customer side, 81% of my
customers are repeat buyers, which tells me retention is doing a lot of
work here."

**4:15–4:45 — Recommendations**
"Based on that, my main recommendations are: cap standard discounts at
20%, take a closer look at Electronics pricing since it's the biggest
category but the weakest margin, and lean into the strength I already
have — a loyal repeat-customer base — with a simple retention program."

**Final — Conclusion**
"Overall, the business is healthy — about 24% profit margin on a loyal
customer base — but there's real room to improve profitability without
even growing sales, just by fixing where the margin is leaking. Thanks for
watching!"
